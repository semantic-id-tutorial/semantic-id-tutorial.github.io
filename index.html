IDs That Mean Something

A hands-on CIKM '26 tutorial on how recommender systems identify items — from arbitrary integer IDs to semantic IDs, for both generative and discriminative recommendation.

📍 Saturday, November 7, 2026 · 14:00–18:30 · Aula 7, Sapienza University of Rome, Faculty of Engineering (San Pietro in Vincoli). Schedule →

Overview

Recommender systems traditionally identify items by arbitrary integer IDs. These IDs carry no meaning, so models must learn everything about an item from its interactions. Semantic IDs build meaning into the identifier itself: each item is represented as a short sequence of discrete tokens derived from its content, so related items share tokens.

This tutorial traces how item identity has evolved from raw IDs, through auxiliary content, to semantic IDs. We review how semantic IDs are constructed and used, and their applications to multimodal, cold-start, and cross-domain recommendation. Two hands-on sessions walk attendees through building semantic IDs, using them in a generative recommender, and transferring them across domains. We close with open challenges such as ID stability as catalogs change and multi-stakeholder utility.

What You Will Learn

  • The evolution from raw IDs to auxiliary content to semantic IDs, and what each step adds.
  • Semantic IDs in generative (e.g., TIGER) and discriminative (e.g., YouTube ranking) recommendation.
  • Multimodality and collaborative signals in semantic IDs, cold-start, and cross-domain transfer.
  • Two hands-on sessions on Amazon Reviews: building semantic IDs, and transferring them across domains.
