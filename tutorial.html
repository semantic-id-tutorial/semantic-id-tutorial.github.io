IDs That Mean Something: A Hands-On Tutorial on Semantic IDs for Recommender Systems

Roger Zhe Li, Aghiles Salah, Mete Sertkan, Changhong Jin, Yingjie Niu, Ruihai Dong, and Barry Smyth. 2026. IDs That Mean Something: A Hands-On Tutorial on Semantic IDs for Recommender Systems. In Proceedings of the 35th ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management (CIKM '26), November 7–11, 2026, Rome, Italy. ACM, New York, NY, USA, 5 pages. https://doi.org/10.1145/3799682.3839274 [[paper]]

Overview

Recommender systems have undergone a significant transformation in how they represent and identify items, evolving from raw IDs as simple lookup keys to semantic IDs as structured interfaces for utilizing and sharing information across recommendation settings. This tutorial reviews that evolution — raw IDs, auxiliary content, and semantic IDs — and their applications across multimodality, cold-start, and cross-domain recommendation, complemented by two hands-on sessions.

Learning Goals

By the end of this tutorial, attendees will:

  1. Have the conceptual grounding and practical tools to apply semantic IDs in their own research or production systems.
  2. Be able to identify scenarios where semantic IDs have the most impact, including cold-start and cross-domain recommendation.
  3. Understand open challenges and emerging directions beyond conventional recommendation, such as dynamic semantic IDs, multi-stakeholder utility, and semantic planning.

Target Audience

This tutorial targets researchers and practitioners with a general interest in recommender systems, information retrieval, and representation learning — particularly those working on knowledge representation and scalable machine learning.

Prerequisites:

  • Basic familiarity with machine learning concepts and recommender systems fundamentals.
  • No prior knowledge of semantic IDs or generative retrieval is required.

Tutorial Outline and Structure

The half-day tutorial consists of 3 hours of core content, plus two coffee breaks. All start times are tentative and will be finalized once CIKM '26 releases the official program.

Time (tentative) Section Presenter
9:00–9:15 Introduction to recommender systems, raw item IDs, and auxiliary content
9:15–9:30 Semantic IDs: introduction & overview
9:30–9:50 Leveraging semantic IDs in recommender systems
9:50–10:05 Coffee break
10:05–10:30 Hands-on: building and using semantic IDs
10:30–10:55 Multimodality and collaborative signals
10:55–11:20 Cold-start mitigation and cross-domain transfer
11:20–11:35 Coffee break
11:35–12:00 Hands-on: cross-domain usage of semantic IDs
12:00–12:15 Future directions and open challenges
12:15–12:30 Q&A and open discussion

ℹ️ Exact session date, start time, and per-section presenter assignments will be announced once CIKM '26 releases the official program.

Format: both hands-on sessions use the Amazon Reviews dataset, with instructions and code provided as self-contained Jupyter notebooks that run on free-tier resources such as Google Colab. Content-based item embeddings and baseline models are pre-computed so the sessions stay focused on semantic IDs. Attendees should bring their own laptop for the hands-on sessions; presentation requires an LCD projector, at least one projection screen, and microphones.
