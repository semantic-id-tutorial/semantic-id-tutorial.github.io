Overview

Recommender systems have long identified items by raw integer IDs. Semantic IDs instead derive each item's identifier from its content. This tutorial covers raw IDs, auxiliary content, and semantic IDs in turn, then their use in multimodal, cold-start, and cross-domain recommendation. Two hands-on sessions accompany the lectures.

Learning Goals

By the end of this tutorial, attendees will:

  1. Have the conceptual grounding and practical tools to apply semantic IDs in their own research or production systems.
  2. Be able to identify scenarios where semantic IDs have the most impact, including cold-start and cross-domain recommendation.
  3. Understand open challenges and emerging directions beyond conventional recommendation, such as dynamic semantic IDs, multi-stakeholder utility, and semantic planning.

Target Audience

This tutorial is for researchers and practitioners working on recommender systems, information retrieval, or representation learning, including those in knowledge representation and scalable machine learning.

Prerequisites:

  • Basic familiarity with machine learning concepts and recommender systems fundamentals.
  • No prior knowledge of semantic IDs or generative retrieval is required.

Tutorial Outline and Structure

CIKM '26 · Saturday, November 7, 2026 · 14:00–18:30 · Aula 7, Sapienza University of Rome, Faculty of Engineering (San Pietro in Vincoli). Directions. The half-day tutorial consists of 4 hours of content, plus a 30-minute tea break.

Time Section Presenter
14:00–14:25 Introduction to recommender systems, raw item IDs, and auxiliary content Ruihai Dong
14:25–14:50 Semantic IDs: introduction & overview Roger Zhe Li
14:50–15:25 Leveraging semantic IDs in recommender systems Roger Zhe Li
15:25–16:00 Hands-on: building and using semantic IDs Aghiles Salah

16:30–17:00 Multimodality and collaborative signals Mete Sertkan
17:00–17:30 Cold-start mitigation and cross-domain transfer Mete Sertkan

18:00–18:15 Future directions and open challenges Roger Zhe Li
18:15–18:30 Q&A and open discussion All presenters

Format: both hands-on sessions use the Amazon Reviews dataset, with instructions and code provided as self-contained Jupyter notebooks that run on free-tier resources such as Google Colab. Content-based item embeddings and baseline models are pre-computed so the sessions stay focused on semantic IDs. Please bring your own laptop for the hands-on sessions.
