Roger Zhe Li, et al. (2026). IDs That Mean Something: A Hands-On Tutorial on Semantic IDs for Recommender Systems. CIKM '26. https://doi.org/10.1145/3799682.3839274 [[tutorial]]

The list starts from TIGER (NeurIPS '23), the paper that made generating semantic IDs a recommendation paradigm. It covers 130+ papers, grouped to follow the tutorial outline (Sections 2.3–2.7). Each paper links to its official venue version (or arXiv) and to code where available. We keep the list up to date, and contributions are welcome through pull requests.

Background: From Raw IDs to Semantic IDs

Arbitrary integer IDs, content as side input, and why item identity needed to change.

Construction & Tokenization

Quantization and tokenization architectures that turn item content into discrete token sequences.

Generative Recommendation

Semantic IDs as the backbone of generative retrieval and recommendation, starting from TIGER.

Discriminative Ranking & Retrieval

Semantic IDs as structured input features for ranking, CTR prediction, and embedding-based retrieval.

Multimodality

Fusing text, image, audio, and video into semantic ID construction.

Collaborative Signals

Aligning content-derived IDs with collaborative and behavioral signals.

Cold-Start

Using shared semantic structure to recommend new and long-tail items.

Cross-Domain Transfer

Transferring knowledge across domains and platforms through semantic IDs.

Cross-Task Unification & Planning

One representation for recommendation, search, and other tasks.

Surveys & Tutorials

Surveys and tutorials on generative recommendation and semantic IDs.
